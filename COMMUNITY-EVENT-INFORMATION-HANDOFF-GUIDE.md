# Community Event Information Handoff Guide

When a community event appears on a chamber calendar, social post, organization website, and registration service, each surface should help people reach the same next step with confidence.

This short guide is for churches, nonprofits, venues, and local businesses coordinating a public event across several channels.

## Start with one source of truth

Choose one owned page as the current home for the event. Keep the date, time, location, audience, cost, and primary action there. Other announcements can stay brief and point people to that page.

## Make the next step unmistakable

Decide what you want a visitor to do:

- register or purchase;
- volunteer;
- sponsor;
- add the event to a calendar; or
- contact the right team.

Give the primary action a plain label and keep secondary actions nearby without competing with it.

## Keep handoffs consistent

Before publishing, compare the owned event page, outside calendar listing, registration page, and contact details. Confirm that they use the same event name, date, location, and destination. When details change, update the owned page first and then the other channels.

## Give every step an owner

Name the person or team responsible for event details, registration, sponsor questions, and day-of updates. A shared role inbox can help route questions when several people support the event.

## Plan the full event window

Before the event, make registration and reminders easy to find. During the event, keep directions and contact information prominent. Afterward, replace expired calls to action with a thank-you, a recap, or the next relevant way to participate.

## Measure real responses

Track completed registrations, confirmed volunteer or sponsor inquiries, consultation requests, and direct replies separately. Treat views and clicks as navigation signals, not as attendees, donors, customers, or revenue.
