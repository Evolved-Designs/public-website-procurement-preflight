# Texas Residential Solar Web and Sales Handoff Readiness Checklist

Updated September 2, 2026.

Texas residential solar retailers and salespersons entered a new registration and enforcement phase on September 1, 2026. This checklist helps an operator, counsel, compliance owner, and web team trace approved facts across the public site, lead capture, salesperson handoff, contract delivery, cancellation path, and retained evidence.

This is an operational planning aid, not legal advice or a compliance certification. The controlling statute, rules, TDLR instructions, exemptions, and a qualified Texas attorney determine what applies. Do not treat a completed checklist as permission to sell, lease, advertise, or contract.

## Confirm the current rule state before changing the site

Record the review date and link every decision to the current official source.

- TDLR says residential solar retailers and salespersons generally must be registered to conduct covered sales or leases beginning September 1, 2026.
- TDLR began accepting online applications on August 10, 2026.
- TDLR announced that enforcement of 16 TAC Sections 71.41 and 71.43 is temporarily suspended until November 1, 2026. Do not infer that registration or other requirements are suspended.
- The adopted Chapter 71 rules cover registration, insurance, renewals, exemptions, contracts, disclosures, educational materials, conduct, supervision, fees, and enforcement.
- Applicability and exemptions require legal review. A marketing label such as installer, dealer, consultant, or contractor does not decide the legal classification.

## Assign the five controlling owners

Do not let the web team invent compliance facts. Name one accountable owner for each row.

| Decision owner | Owns | Release evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Legal/compliance | Applicability, exemptions, required language, disclosure timing, cancellation, prohibited acts | Dated written approval and source links |
| Registration owner | Retailer and salesperson records, responsible party, insurance, renewals, change notices | TDLR record references and renewal calendar |
| Sales operations | Lead routing, salesperson assignment, scripts, supervision, handoff timing | Test leads, approved scripts, roster match |
| Web/content | Public claims, identity, contact routes, forms, accessibility, versioned pages | URL inventory, screenshots, form receipts, change log |
| Records/security | Contract and disclosure delivery, consent, retention, access, deletion, incident handling | Data map, access list, retention schedule, restore test |

## Run the 16-field fact and handoff check

For each applicable field, compare the approved source with the public page, form, CRM, salesperson script, contract workflow, and confirmation message. Mark `match`, `conflict`, `missing`, or `not applicable`; never count an unchecked field as a match.

| # | Field | Evidence to compare | Fail-safe action |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Legal business identity | Entity name, assumed name, retailer application, footer and form owner | Stop release when identities cannot be reconciled |
| 2 | Registration state | TDLR retailer record and effective/renewal dates | Do not publish an unverified registered claim |
| 3 | Salesperson association | Current salesperson roster and supervising retailer | Block assignment when the roster does not match |
| 4 | Applicability/exemption | Counsel-approved scope and cited authority | Route ambiguous classifications to counsel |
| 5 | Consumer contact | Approved phone, mailing address, email or website route | Repair dead, vendor-owned, or unmonitored paths |
| 6 | Responsible party | Record keeper and continuity owner | Test coverage during absence or cessation |
| 7 | Insurance fact | Current approved certificate facts | Never translate coverage into a consumer guarantee |
| 8 | Installer fact | Approved electrical contractor name and license evidence | Do not guess or preselect an unapproved installer |
| 9 | Offer and savings claims | Source data, assumptions, expiration and approval | Remove unsupported savings, scarcity, rebate, or endorsement claims |
| 10 | Lead consent | Purpose, recipient, channel, optionality and timestamp | Do not bundle marketing consent into an unrelated request |
| 11 | Disclosure handoff | Current department form, language, completion and signature state | Prevent contract progression when the approved gate fails |
| 12 | Educational material | Current department material and delivery evidence | Preserve the official version and delivery timestamp |
| 13 | Contract delivery | Executed copy, delivery route and timestamp | Surface a recoverable copy; do not rely on a salesperson device |
| 14 | Cancellation route | Approved deadline calculation and written-notice address or email | Test the route and escalation without sending a real cancellation |
| 15 | Complaint/support route | Monitored ownership, response clock and escalation | Prevent abandoned inboxes and silent form failures |
| 16 | Change control | Approver, effective date, affected pages and rollback | Re-review after a rule, roster, offer, vendor or workflow change |

Use one denominator:

`parity rate = matching applicable fields / applicable fields x 100`

Report conflicts separately. A high percentage must not hide a failed registration, salesperson, disclosure, contract, cancellation, or deceptive-claim gate.

## Test the visitor-to-salesperson journey

Use synthetic data clearly labeled as a test. Do not submit a consumer credit application, authorize a hard inquiry, sign a contract, impersonate a consumer, or enter a real government application.

1. Start on every indexed landing page, location page, advertisement destination, profile link, and campaign URL.
2. Confirm the page identifies the operator and distinguishes the retailer, salesperson, installer, lender, utility, and government agency where those roles are mentioned.
3. Check that savings, tax, rebate, financing, approval, timeline, warranty, and utility statements have a named owner, dated basis, and required qualification.
4. Submit one synthetic inquiry per materially different form route.
5. Verify the receipt, CRM owner, salesperson association, consent evidence, and non-marketing service message.
6. Walk the approved disclosure, educational-material, contract-copy, and cancellation-support handoffs with counsel or the compliance owner.
7. Repeat with keyboard-only navigation, 200% zoom, a narrow viewport, and a screen-reader smoke test.
8. Remove test data according to the approved retention rule and save the deletion evidence.

## Release gates

Release only when every applicable gate has an owner and evidence.

- **Gate 1 — Authority:** counsel has classified the workflow and approved the current sources, exemptions, language, and enforcement-date interpretation.
- **Gate 2 — Registration:** retailer and salesperson records, association, responsible party, insurance, renewal, and change-notice owners match the approved record.
- **Gate 3 — Truthful discovery:** public identity, offers, installer roles, savings assumptions, financing roles, contact facts, and campaign claims are supported and dated.
- **Gate 4 — Protected handoff:** consent, disclosure, educational-material, contract-copy, cancellation, complaint, retention, and access paths pass without using real consumer data.
- **Gate 5 — Accessible recovery:** a consumer can find, understand, revisit, and use the important paths with keyboard and assistive technology, and staff can recover a failed handoff.
- **Gate 6 — Rollback:** the team has the before-state, approved change set, release time, owner, rollback method, and next official-source review date.

## Measure outcomes without overstating them

Keep discovery, operational completion, and compliance evidence separate.

| Measure | Definition | Do not call it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualified inquiry rate | Valid test- or privacy-safe inquiries divided by eligible sessions | A signed contract or compliant sale |
| Assigned-roster match | Inquiries routed to an approved associated salesperson divided by routed inquiries | Proof every salesperson is registered |
| Handoff completion | Workflows reaching the approved next state divided by attempts | Consumer understanding or legal compliance |
| Cancellation-route recovery | Synthetic tests acknowledged and escalated within the approved time | A legal cancellation test |
| Fact conflict count | Material conflicts across the 16-field check | A violation count |
| Evidence freshness | Applicable records reviewed within the approved interval | Permanent approval |

Do not expose consumer financial, identity, contract, complaint, or cancellation data in analytics. Treat a click, form view, phone tap, downloaded document, or CRM status as only the event it actually proves.
