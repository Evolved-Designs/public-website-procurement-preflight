# AI Search Program RFP Requirements Checklist

A buyer-side planning aid for public-service teams procuring an answer engine optimization (AEO), generative engine optimization (GEO), or AI-search visibility program.

Use this checklist before release to turn an emerging service category into measurable work, explicit controls, and evidence-based acceptance. It is vendor-neutral and does not promise placement, citation, ranking, traffic, or inclusion in any AI experience.

## 1. Define the public outcome

- [ ] Name the audiences and public questions the program must serve.
- [ ] Identify the authoritative pages, datasets, policies, programs, people, and locations that answer those questions.
- [ ] Separate accuracy, discoverability, citation, referral traffic, and completed public-service actions into distinct outcomes.
- [ ] State which languages, regions, departments, and digital properties are in scope.
- [ ] Define what is out of scope, including paid media, fabricated third-party mentions, review manipulation, and guaranteed placement.
- [ ] Require the vendor to explain how AI-search work fits the organization's existing SEO, accessibility, content, analytics, privacy, and records programs.

**Acceptance evidence:** approved outcome map, in-scope property inventory, audience-and-question register, and named owners.

## 2. Establish a reproducible baseline

- [ ] Create a versioned panel of representative public questions before implementation begins.
- [ ] Record the exact question, locale, platform, date, account state, and observation method for every baseline check.
- [ ] Capture whether the organization is named, whether an official source is cited, whether the answer is accurate, and whether the citation resolves.
- [ ] Preserve screenshots or exports only when permitted by platform terms and organizational policy.
- [ ] Inventory crawl, indexation, canonical, redirect, robots, sitemap, structured-data, and JavaScript-rendering conditions for priority pages.
- [ ] Record first-party search and analytics baselines without treating a third-party visibility score as an official platform metric.

**Acceptance evidence:** timestamped baseline dataset, method note, technical inventory, and known limitations.

## 3. Require platform-specific controls

- [ ] Require a crawler-control matrix that distinguishes search discovery, model training, and user-agent behavior.
- [ ] For ChatGPT search discovery, document the intended policy for `OAI-SearchBot` separately from the policy for `GPTBot`.
- [ ] For Google generative search features, require indexed, snippet-eligible pages and compliance with normal Search technical and content guidance.
- [ ] For Microsoft experiences, require Bing Webmaster Tools access and a plan to use available citation, cited-page, and grounding-query data.
- [ ] Require change approval before modifying robots directives, `noindex`, canonical tags, structured data, or sitemap behavior.
- [ ] Require rollback instructions and a verification step for every technical change.

**Acceptance evidence:** approved crawler matrix, before/after technical checks, change log, and tested rollback record.

## 4. Protect accuracy and authority

- [ ] Assign an accountable subject-matter owner for each priority topic.
- [ ] Require source citations for factual claims, statistics, eligibility rules, dates, fees, and public instructions.
- [ ] Define review frequency based on the risk and volatility of the information.
- [ ] Require correction and escalation workflows for inaccurate AI-generated answers discovered during monitoring.
- [ ] Prohibit fabricated expertise, synthetic testimonials, undisclosed impersonation, and inauthentic third-party amplification.
- [ ] Require consistency across official web pages, profiles, directories, contact details, and structured data.

**Acceptance evidence:** content authority matrix, approval log, source register, correction playbook, and sampled accuracy review.

## 5. Specify content work without prescribing hype

- [ ] Require unique, helpful, people-first content that answers the public's real questions.
- [ ] Ask vendors to identify gaps in clarity, completeness, structure, freshness, and evidence before proposing new pages.
- [ ] Require plain-language headings, descriptive links, meaningful tables only where comparison helps, and accessible alternatives for non-text content.
- [ ] Require the vendor to distinguish tested practices from hypotheses.
- [ ] Do not require unsupported tactics such as mass AI-generated pages, inauthentic mentions, or unnecessary AI-only files.
- [ ] Require human review and disclosure practices appropriate to the organization's generative-content policy.

**Acceptance evidence:** approved content briefs, source-backed drafts, accessibility review, editorial approvals, and change history.

## 6. Make accessibility a program gate

- [ ] State the applicable accessibility standard and organizational policy.
- [ ] Require semantic structure, keyboard access, descriptive controls, accessible forms, and usable alternative text.
- [ ] Include assistive-technology and accessibility-tree checks for priority user journeys.
- [ ] Require remediation ownership, severity definitions, and response targets.
- [ ] Treat accessibility as both a public obligation and a technical input to browser agents; do not reduce it to an AI-search tactic.

**Acceptance evidence:** accessibility conformance report or equivalent evidence, issue log, remediation verification, and exception approvals.

## 7. Define privacy, security, and records boundaries

- [ ] Prohibit collection of personal or sensitive prompt data unless it is necessary, approved, and documented.
- [ ] Define retention, access, deletion, export, and breach-notification requirements for program data.
- [ ] Require an inventory of third-party tools, processors, model providers, and data destinations.
- [ ] State whether prompts, outputs, screenshots, and evaluation datasets are public records or otherwise subject to retention rules.
- [ ] Require least-privilege access, multifactor authentication, account ownership, and credential handoff.
- [ ] Prohibit publishing confidential or predecisional material to test AI systems.

**Acceptance evidence:** data-flow diagram, tool register, access list, retention schedule, and approved security/privacy review.

## 8. Make measurement auditable

- [ ] Report platform-provided metrics separately from vendor-created estimates or composite scores.
- [ ] Track accuracy, official-source citation, citation resolution, cited-page coverage, referral sessions, and downstream actions as separate measures.
- [ ] Preserve the fixed question panel while adding a separately labeled discovery panel for new questions.
- [ ] Define sampling frequency, rerun rules, variance notes, and how logged-in or personalized results are handled.
- [ ] Require annotations for content, technical, platform, and measurement-method changes.
- [ ] Do not accept a single blended score without its formula, raw observations, limitations, and export rights.

**Acceptance evidence:** metric dictionary, raw export, reproducible calculation, annotated dashboard, and monthly finding-to-action log.

## 9. Set deliverables and acceptance gates

Require, at minimum:

- [ ] discovery and baseline package;
- [ ] technical findings with prioritized fixes and rollback steps;
- [ ] content and entity accuracy plan;
- [ ] approved implementation backlog with owners and dependencies;
- [ ] monitoring dashboard plus raw-data export;
- [ ] monthly accuracy and citation review;
- [ ] training for content, web, analytics, and communications owners;
- [ ] incident, correction, and platform-change playbooks;
- [ ] final documentation, credentials, source files, and data handoff;
- [ ] 30-, 60-, and 90-day post-handoff verification.

Tie payment or phase completion to accepted evidence, not to untestable promises.

## 10. Ask vendors to prove fit

Ask each respondent to provide:

- [ ] two relevant examples with the baseline, intervention, observation period, and measurable result;
- [ ] one example where a proposed tactic failed or produced no clear change, plus what the team learned;
- [ ] a sample raw-data export and metric dictionary;
- [ ] the names and roles of the people who will perform the work;
- [ ] the tools and external services they expect to use;
- [ ] their method for separating correlation from causation;
- [ ] their approach to platform changes and discontinued metrics;
- [ ] their accessibility, privacy, security, and records practices;
- [ ] all assumptions, buyer dependencies, exclusions, and optional costs;
- [ ] confirmation that no ranking, citation, or inclusion guarantee is being offered.

## 11. Use a defensible evaluation structure

Apply pass/fail gates before scoring:

1. complete response and signed attestations;
2. conflict and legal disclosures;
3. privacy, security, accessibility, and records commitments;
4. ownership and export rights;
5. no deceptive or guaranteed-placement practices;
6. acceptance of required evidence and reporting.

Then score only published factors, such as:

- problem understanding and public outcome alignment;
- measurement rigor and reproducibility;
- technical and content approach;
- accuracy and governance controls;
- accessibility, privacy, security, and records fit;
- relevant delivery evidence;
- implementation and handoff quality;
- total evaluated cost.

Preserve an evidence note for every rating and a documented consensus rationale.

## 12. Plan exit and continuity before award

- [ ] Confirm the organization owns all approved content, configurations, datasets, exports, dashboards, and documentation paid for under the contract.
- [ ] Require export in documented, non-proprietary formats where practical.
- [ ] Define credential transfer, access removal, retention/deletion confirmation, and transition support.
- [ ] Require enough documentation for another qualified team to continue measurement and maintenance.
- [ ] State how active experiments and unresolved corrections will be handed off.

**Acceptance evidence:** completed handoff checklist, validated exports, access-removal record, and continuity runbook.

## Procurement notes

- Verify the final solicitation against your organization's legal, procurement, accessibility, privacy, security, and records requirements.
- Keep vendor questions and answers in the official procurement channel.
- When official platform guidance changes, version the requirement and record the decision rather than silently changing the scoring standard.
