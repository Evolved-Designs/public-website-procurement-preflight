# Local Event Inquiry Follow-Up Planner

A simple one-page guide for busy venue and event-business owners who want inquiries to feel welcoming, clear, and easy to answer.

## Before the busy season

Write down the few details your team needs before it can give a useful answer:

- event type and preferred date
- estimated guest count
- the service, room, or package the customer is considering
- the best way and time to reply
- any deadline the customer is working toward

Keep the first conversation light. The goal is to help someone take the next step, not make them complete a long application.

## Give every inquiry an owner

Decide who answers each kind of question and what happens when that person is away. A shared inbox can work well when one person is responsible for checking it and another person is the clear backup.

A short acknowledgement helps the customer know the message arrived. It can simply confirm the expected response window and point to current pricing, availability, or visit information when that is useful.

## Make handoffs easy

When an inquiry moves from the website to a call, tour, estimate, or booking tool, carry forward the details the customer already shared. Asking for the same information again creates avoidable friction.

Use plain status labels your whole team understands, such as:

- new
- replied
- waiting on customer
- consultation scheduled
- not a fit
- complete

## Review what customers actually ask

Once a month, look at the questions that required the most back-and-forth. If the same question appears repeatedly, answer it where customers naturally look for it. Keep dates, pricing language, contact details, and availability guidance current across the places you control.

Measure completed conversations and scheduled consultations separately from page views or clicks. A view is interest; it is not yet an inquiry or a booking.
